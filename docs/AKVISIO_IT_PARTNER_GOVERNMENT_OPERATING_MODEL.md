# AKVISIO — IT Partner — Government Operating Model

**Purpose:** negotiation baseline for preparing RT/RW-SID CONNECT for a prospective public-service program. Not a binding agreement, procurement approval, government appointment or legal opinion.

## 1. Proposed roles

### AKVISIO — product originator / rights holder, subject to verification
- Provide product vision, product history, repositories and contribution evidence.
- Identify pre-existing code, brand, documentation, datasets, open-source components and third-party services.
- Define sought license, attribution, permitted uses, derivative-work rules and commercial participation, subject to chain-of-title review.
- Approve material product/IP changes through agreed governance.

### IT partner — engineering, implementation and service operator
- Perform due diligence, secure development, remediation, infrastructure design, testing, deployment, monitoring, helpdesk, training and continuity.
- Produce source, migrations, architecture diagrams, SBOM, test reports, runbooks, backup/restore evidence and release notes.
- Operate only under approved access controls and data-processing instructions; never reuse or disclose public-service data.
- Maintain an exit/transition package to avoid vendor lock-in.

### Government — prospective program sponsor/authority/operator
- Determine policy, legal mandate, service ownership, official data purposes, agency responsibilities and standards through formal processes.
- Approve regional roles, integrations, retention, service levels, incident communication, funding and acceptance.
- Determine procurement/partnership pathway and applicable requirements.
- Government participation remains prospective until written authorization/agreement exists.

### Residents
- Access agreed core citizen services free of charge.
- Receive clear notices and workable correction, complaint and assistance channels.
- Resident subscription is not the assumed funding model for the national core service.

## 2. Separate four kinds of rights

1. Software/IP: pre-existing software, new code, modifications, designs, documentation, marks and third-party components.
2. Government usage/program rights: territory, term, users, agencies, purposes, source access, continuity, customization, sublicensing and transition.
3. Data rights/duties: service data, controller/processor roles, custody, access, reuse, export, retention, disclosure and deletion.
4. Commercial/economic rights: implementation fee, operations/support fee, license fee, royalty, reporting, audit and tax.

Government operation does not automatically transfer software IP. Software ownership does not automatically authorize processing government/resident data.

## 3. Commercial principle

- Core program access: **Rp0 to residents**.
- Delivery/operating costs: funded through separately approved program/partner arrangements; Rp0 initial capital is a negotiation target, not a guarantee that work and operations have no cost.
- AKVISIO compensation may be license fee, royalty, fixed/milestone fee or a negotiated combination. Do not fix a rate before identifying payer, revenue base, rights and costs.
- If royalty is used, define gross/net base, deductions, collection timing, reporting, audit, underpayment interest, tax, related-party transactions, public-sector restrictions and termination.
- Avoid automatic exclusivity. Review territory, duration, minimum commitments, measurable targets, remedies and termination/transition rights.
- No guaranteed revenue, government approval or sole-source procurement is assumed.

## 4. Due diligence package

Before definitive agreement:
- source/commit history; contribution and assignment agreements; contractor/employee IP assignments where relevant;
- software bill of materials and license scan; open-source/commercial component obligations;
- trademark/domain ownership and clearance;
- architecture/security/privacy assessment, penetration-test plan and remediation tracker;
- data inventory, data-flow map, threat model, backup/restore evidence and incident runbook;
- feature inventory separating implemented, partial, demo-only, disabled and planned features;
- test reports and acceptance criteria;
- total cost of ownership for hosting, support, security, operations, training and rollout;
- service-level objectives, support matrix, continuity and exit/transition plan.

## 5. Term-sheet topics

1. Parties, authority to negotiate, definitions and scope.
2. Existing IP schedule and chain-of-title conditions precedent.
3. License grant, territory, term, users, purpose, modification, source access, sublicensing and attribution.
4. Ownership/licensing of new code, improvements, integrations, configuration and documentation.
5. Program governance, steering committee and change control.
6. Controller/processor roles, lawful instructions, security schedule, hosting/subprocessors and data-sharing.
7. Milestones, acceptance tests, defects, warranty limits and change requests.
8. Operating fees, funding source, payment schedule, royalty base/rate if any, reporting and audit.
9. Incidents, breach cooperation, service levels, liability, indemnities and insurance as appropriate.
10. Procurement/compliance conditions, conflicts and anti-corruption controls.
11. Termination, transition, source/data export, credential handover, deletion certification and continuity.
12. Dispute resolution, governing law, confidentiality and public disclosure obligations.
13. Conditions precedent: legal review, procurement/partnership approval, security acceptance, privacy assessment and funding approval.

## 6. Negotiation sequence

1. NDA and controlled demo.
2. Technical, security, privacy and IP due diligence.
3. Agree target architecture and remediation backlog.
4. Agree non-binding term sheet with assumptions and unresolved items.
5. Validate legal/procurement pathway with qualified counsel and prospective government stakeholders.
6. Run limited pilot with synthetic or formally authorized data and written success criteria.
7. Negotiate definitive agreements after pilot findings, costs, responsibilities and rights are evidenced.

## 7. Red lines

- Do not represent the app as government-adopted before formal approval.
- Do not claim national capacity or security certification without independent evidence.
- Do not transfer all IP, grant exclusivity or promise a royalty rate before rights/economics review.
- Do not use real citizen data in partner demos without lawful authorization and protections.
- Do not enable AI writes to sensitive records without authorization, audit and human approval.
- Do not charge residents for core participation under this proposed model.
- Do not launch nationally before security, privacy, operations and acceptance gates are signed off.
